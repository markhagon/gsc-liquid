Sugar & Spice and all things science

Most people think sugar is a bowl of white stuff to sweeten tea and cakes, but it’s more complex than that. There are thousands of sugars. Regular table sugar is sucrose, a combination of glucose and fructose, but there’s also lactose, maltose, dextrose, ribose, trehalose… literally thousands. 

So why do we use Allulose in our chocolate?

Allulose is a rare, natural sugar found in figs, raisins and maple syrup. Humans can't fully metabolize it, so, it doesn't raise blood glucose or insulin levels.

I’m no expert in explaining metabolic dysfunction and the various mechanisms that lead to chronic diseases, I’m just a cook. But these experts know a thing or two...

Dr. Peter Attia

Dr. Peter Attia’s perspective on sugar centers on its role in metabolic dysfunction, specifically its impact on liver health, insulin resistance, and the development of chronic diseases like Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD). He differentiates sugar based on metabolic impact rather than whether it is "natural" or "refined"

Jessie Inchauspe

Jessie Inchauspe, known as the "Glucose Goddess," is a biochemist and author who promotes simple, science-backed hacks to flatten blood sugar curves. Her techniques focus on reducing glucose spikes to prevent inflammation, fatigue, cravings, and hormonal imbalances. Core advice includes eating foods in order (fiber first), savory breakfasts, and moving after meals
